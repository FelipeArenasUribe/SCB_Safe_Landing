The paper combines four elements needed for position-and-attitude landing control on a small celestial body.

Abstract

Landing on small celestial bodies is challenging because the spacecraft must satisfy safety and actuator constraints despite gravitational forces that are difficult to model accurately. This article presents an optimal control for safe landing with gravitational uncertainty and strict actuator limits.

The approach addresses spacecraft pose control with attitude represented on SO(3), where there are constraints on position, attitude, velocity, angular velocity, and the actuators. An extended high-gain observer estimates the gravitational uncertainty and provides a dynamic upper bound on the estimation error. A composite control barrier function is then used to compute optimal forces and torques that satisfy the state and actuator constraints while tracking a landing trajectory. Finally, an optimal allocation maps the forces and torques to feasible actuator commands. The method is demonstrated in simulation using two vehicle configurations with severe gravitational uncertainty.

01 · Problem

Constrained landing problem

The spacecraft must approach the touchdown site inside a safe corridor, maintain sensor visibility, respect speed limits, and never request more than its actuators can deliver.

02 · Method

Control architecture

The proposed controller combines disturbance estimation, nominal trajectory tracking, safety enforcement, and control allocation.

1

Polytope projection

Map the high-dimensional actuator set into an exact polytope of admissible forces and torques.

2

Disturbance estimation

Estimate gravitational uncertainty online and update a bound on the residual estimation error.

3

Composite barrier filter

Use a composite barrier constraint to find the closest safe force-and-torque command.

4

Control allocation

Distribute the safe command across thrusters and reaction wheels without violating individual limits.

Input-constraint transformation

Actuator limits in force-torque space

Polytope projection converts individual actuator bounds into equivalent inequalities on the net force and torque. The safety filter can therefore enforce the true hardware limits directly, before the allocation layer recovers actuator commands.

03 · Results

Simulation study

The numerical study evaluates the controller across two vehicle configurations and two small-body models under severe gravitational uncertainty.

Vehicles
Robotic lander and crewed landing module
Body models
Ellipsoidal and irregular shapes
Actuation
